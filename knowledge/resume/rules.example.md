# Resume Tailoring Rules

## Objective

Preserve one credible identity across applications:

> **Senior Software Engineer with strong production experience across full-stack web development, backend systems, and cloud infrastructure.**

Keep the resume's core story fixed; tailoring reorders and selects, it does not reinvent. Factual accuracy and interview defensibility always outrank ATS/JD keyword coverage.

## Sources and precedence

- `main_resume.md`: canonical submitted wording, structure, titles, dates, and fixed content.
- `base_resume.md`: evidence database for additional facts and fit assessment; it is not resume copy.
- If sources conflict, flag the conflict; never silently choose.

## Fixed content

Keep these parts of `main_resume.md` verbatim: name, contact details, headline, Profile, job titles, dates, education, and languages. Do not paraphrase, shorten, expand, reorder, or inject JD terms into fixed content. Never round up, extrapolate, or reassign a metric.

## Allowed tailoring

- Reorder and select Skills to put the JD's most important supported skills first. Never invent skills or unsupported expertise.
- Select bullets from `base_resume.md`'s evidence bank that add a distinct, relevant signal not already covered — use their wording as written, don't embellish.
- Minor formatting/spacing changes for a clean result.

## Evidence boundaries

- A gap stays a gap; adjacent experience may be described only as transferable.
- Never invent clients, users, revenue, funding, people-management scope, or any unsupported scale.
- Before adding a claim, confirm the candidate can explain what they personally built, why, and the outcome. If evidence is insufficient, omit it.

## Writing and ATS

Use concise engineering language, active verbs, concrete systems, clear ownership, and supported metrics. Avoid buzzwords like "expert," "results-driven," or "rockstar" unless explicitly supported. ATS optimization means recognizable truthful terminology and conventional headings — not mirroring every JD noun.
